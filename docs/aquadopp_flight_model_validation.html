Instrument note · Aquadopp HR on Slocum jane · Sep 2026

The Aquadopp Check

Both Taiwan MicroRiders flew without an EM flowmeter, so their speed comes from a flight model that never sees a measured speed anywhere in its calibration. A 2 MHz Aquadopp HR profiler that flew on the sister glider jane in 2013 measures the flow past the hull directly. It says the model's angle of attack is about 1.6° too small on dives — which makes its speed ~5–6% too high and any ε built on it ~20–25% too low. Climbs agree to 0.3%.

01

Which glider carried it, and on whose clock

The file names no platform. jane is identified by overlap: the Aquadopp record (2013-04-29 05:00Z → 05-04 06:43Z) sits inside the deployment gliders/20130427_jane_tn (04-28 09:45 → 05-04 05:10), and no other 2013 glider record overlaps it. Matching the two pressure series settles both the platform and the clock:

glider_time = aqd_time + 38.0 s   (37.0 → 39.0 s over the record)
glider_P    = 0.600 + 1.0125 · aqd_P   dbar

residual 0.184 dbar · sharpness 117 · 385,489 in-water samples

The offset is estimated the same way as a MicroRider clock: fit the glider's pressure, interpolated at aqd_time + L, against the Aquadopp's own, and minimize the residual. A yo is a sawtooth, so the minimum is sharp rather than the flat valley a monotonic ramp would give.

02

What the file says about itself

Two record fields decide whether the numbers mean anything, and both were read from the file rather than assumed.

The ten-times trap

The status byte (0x62 on every record) carries the velocity scaling in bit 1: set means 0.1 mm/s, not mm/s. Read as mm/s, every velocity is ten times too big — and still looks superficially plausible, which is what makes it dangerous. Bit 0, clear here, says the head looks up. The same byte is the only place either fact appears.

The beam-to-XYZ transformation matrix sits at hdSystem + 8 in the head record — found by scanning the block for the classic Nortek three-beam form rather than trusting an offset from memory:

[[ 1.5774  -0.7891  -0.7891 ]
 [ 0.0000  -1.3662   1.3662 ]
 [ 0.3677   0.3677   0.3677 ]]

The user configuration gives 29 bins, 1 s sampling, 3 beams, and coordinate system 0 — ENU. That last one matters: the instrument has already rotated the velocities with its own compass and tilt, so the wrapping lives underneath a rotation that has to be undone first.

03

The wrapping problem, and why range cannot fix it

Beam velocities saturate at ±0.1285 m/s — the HR ambiguity velocity — while the glider flies at about 0.3 m/s. Every beam wraps once or twice.

The obvious remedy fails. All 29 cells span only ~0.9 m and see nearly the same velocity, so they wrap together: there is no discontinuity in range to unwrap against. Time works instead. Each dive begins at an inflection where the glider's speed passes through zero, and turbulent fluctuations (centimeters per second) are far below the ambiguity, so the phase can be tracked forward.

A blind time-unwrap still slips — tried first, it put three of eight segments on the wrong branch (0.72 m/s at 17° of attack; 0.095 m/s at 55°). So the branch is chosen by a prior instead, built from quantities the two instruments measure:

  1. Take the glider's depth rate dh/dt and pitch, and form U = |dh/dt| / sin(|pitch| + α) with α starting at zero.
  2. Predict the three beam velocities from that, and pick the nearest branch: b = b_meas + 2·Va·round((b_pred − b_meas) / 2·Va).
  3. Let the data set the velocity vector within ±Va, recompute α, and iterate.

Why this is not circular

The prior contains no flight model — only a measured depth rate and a measured pitch. It has to be right to about 0.14 m/s to pick the correct branch, and it is anchored to a measurement, so it cannot manufacture the answer. Tested directly: shifting the prior's pitch by ±1° leaves the recovered α unchanged to 0.01°.

Corroboration from the literature

The measured dive value, 4.3–4.6° at 22–25° of pitch, sits on top of Tanaka et al. (2022)'s ADCP-measured Slocum angle of attack, −4.4° at −23.7° pitch — the only directly measured Slocum value in print. gliderflight's uncalibrated lift/drag defaults give 2.5–3°.

05

What was varied, and what moved

Two things to take from it. The prior does not drive the answer — perturbing its pitch leaves α untouched. And the climb branch is the weak one: started from −6°, the climb lands on a different wrap and reports 0.373 m/s. The dive number is the one to trust, which is also the leg where the discrepancy lives.

06

Systematics — including one that looks alarming and cancels

α is sensitive one-for-one to how the instrument is bolted to the hull: perturbing the attitude used in the un-rotation by 1° moves α by 1°. So the two tilt records were compared directly, over 371,804 samples:

aqd_pitch − glider_pitch = −0.42 − 0.032·pitch − 0.032·roll
aqd_roll  − glider_roll  = +2.56 + 0.035·pitch − 0.226·roll

pitch fit: corr 0.9991 · residual MAD 0.43° · n = 371,804

The dominant term is proportional to pitch — a 3.2% gain difference, which at ±25° is ∓0.8° and flips sign between dive and climb. That is a sensor gain, not a mounting tilt: a tilt would be a constant. The constant part, the actual alignment, is only ~0.2–0.4°.

And the gain does not propagate

The instrument rotated into ENU using its own attitude, and the analysis un-rotates with the same numbers, so a gain error cancels exactly. It would matter only if the glider's attitude were substituted for the instrument's. The obvious geometric alternative is excluded too: a yaw misalignment large enough to explain 3.2% (14.6°) would put a −0.25·pitch term in the roll difference, and the measured slope is +0.035.

What remains: ±0.4° from mounting alignment and ±0.5° from flow distortion near the hull — visible in the table above as α falling from 5.09° in the nearest cells to 4.20° in the far ones. Both are smaller than the 1.6° dive discrepancy, which is why it survives.

07

What this licenses, and what it does not

Not applied to anything

This is jane, 2013-04-29 → 05-04 — not husker (MicroRider MR046, May 2013) and not doug (2017). Same glider class, same mission pattern, comparable speed and pitch, but carrying the correction across is an assumption, not a measurement. The MicroRider products ship with their modeled speed unchanged, and both flight READMEs point here.

Three further limits. The dive/climb asymmetry in the measured α (4.55° against 2.99°) may be real — a Slocum trims differently on the two legs — or a residual wrap-branch effect on climbs; this does not resolve it. Nothing here validates the absolute speed scale of the MicroRider products; it bounds one term, the angle of attack, that the flight model otherwise guesses. And turbulence from the Aquadopp itself was not attempted: at ε = 10−8 W/kg the velocity difference across 0.5 m is ~3 mm/s against ~0.1 m/s of single-ping noise, so only very energetic water would register — an estimate, not a tested threshold.

Reproducing it

Everything lives beside the data, in /Volumes/SeaChest/Taiwan/Taiwan13/aquadopp/analysis/.

read_prf.pyNortek reader. Record layout verified against the file; scaling and orientation from the status byte.
beams.pyUndoes the instrument's ENU rotation → XYZ → beam.
aoa.pyPressure-anchored unwrapping; solves speed and angle of attack.
dinkum-flight.yamlFlight-model inputs for jane, on the flight computer's clock.
make_flight_speed_jane.pyThe same Merckelbach recipe used for the MicroRider gliders.
compare.pyMeasured against modeled — the tables above.
dinkum-hotel build -c dinkum-flight.yaml     # jane flight inputs
uv run make_flight_speed_jane.py calibrate  # ~20 min, resumable
uv run make_flight_speed_jane.py run
python compare.py                           # Aquadopp vs model
