The shape of the problem

The MicroRider measures shear and temperature gradient at 512 Hz and knows nothing about salinity, position, or its own through-water speed. The glider knows all three, at 1 Hz, on a different clock. Everything below exists to join them without inventing data at the seam.

Reconnaissance before configuration

Never write a config against the deployment narrative. Three commands settle what is actually on disk:

Two builds, because dinkum-hotel writes one time dimension and the two uses want different bases:

Do not fold the flight channels into hotel.nc. The CTD clock stops between yos and while the CTD is unpumped, so projecting attitude onto it discards exactly the surface intervals and inflections the diagnosis needs.

Five traps, all of which bit

Trap 1 — the .ebd / .ecd duplicate

The glider wrote only LZ4 *.ecd; SFMC then expanded each to a *.ebd and left both. sci/ holds 142 unique segments in 280 files. The default patterns read 138 of them twice. Every CTD sample arrives as an exact duplicate timestamp, time.dedupe: mean collapses them silently, and the hotel file looks perfect while every sample-count and duty-cycle diagnostic reads double. 

patterns: ["flt/*.dbd", "flt/*.dcd", "sci/*.ebd"]   # .ebd is the superset
Trap 2 — NMEA coordinates

Slocum reports every coordinate as ddmm.mmmm. m_lat = 2015.61159 is 20°15.61159′ = 20.260193 °N, not 20.1561. No scale factor converts it, and the raw form must not be interpolated — it steps 40.02 across a whole minute. Use transform: nmea_degrees, which runs on the source samples before projection.

Validation that this is right: the Guam bench files land at 13.433 °N, 144.660 °E — Guam — and the deployment at 20.3 °N, 129.9 °E.

Trap 3 — valid_min/max are in SOURCE units

The range check runs before scale, even though the neighbouring units: names the output unit. Conductivity bounds are S/m; pressure bounds are bar. A bound written in output units is not an error — it is a check that silently never fires.

Trap 4 — sentinels sit inside oceanographic ranges

Slocum writes exactly 0.0 for "never set". A valid_min: -2.0 on temperature let three such samples through, each of which would have produced a nonsense salinity. The coldest real water here is 5.03 °C, so valid_min: 1.0 removes the sentinel and nothing else.

Trap 5 — corrupt files exist

Two science segments hold garbage where the ASCII header belongs — dUZUUUUUUU instead of dbd_label: — in both the .ecd and its .ebd expansion, which is what rules out a bad decompression. A known TWR firmware corruption bug. Name them in files.exclude; do not raise max_skipped, which would also swallow the next new one.

Backend

xarray-dbd, pinned. dbd2netCDF 1.7.5 refuses the mixed flight+science list, and its --sensorOutput filter is broken independently: it indexes the kept-sensor array with the file's sensor index, so any record naming a sensor beyond the kept count aborts the run — while blaming the sensor cache, which is fine.

Trim

perturb trim -o proc/results --p-file-root microrider "microrider/*.p"

255 files, 0 trimmed, all "referenced original in place". A MicroRider shut down cleanly rarely leaves a partial record; the step is cheap insurance, and when it does nothing the raw directory is the trimmed input.

04

The FP07 calibration that is not optional

Both thermistors carried Rockland's nominal coefficients — beta_1 = 3143.55, beta_2 = 2.5e5, t_0 = 289.301 — identical on both probes, with a blank cal_date. Two different glass beads cannot share a calibration.

T1 · SN T2809+4.45 Kerror the factory coefficients carried
T2 · SN T2810+8.15 K…and 3.7 K of disagreement between the two probes
In-situ residual5.2 / 6.7 mKRMS, order 2, over 5.04–26.15 °C

This is normal, not an anomaly — stock FP07 specs have been seen 30 K out. The in-situ fit against the glider's pumped SBE-41CP is the only calibration these probes will ever have.

Diagnostics that matter more than the RMS

  • Lag gate passed (peak width 0.8 s). The gate is on peak sharpness, never on r: a glider dive is a monotonic ramp, and a shifted straight line is the same line plus a constant, so raw correlation sits near 1.0 at every lag.
  • Order 2, chosen by held-out error — not in-sample fit. Order 1 gives 50.8 mK held out, order 2 gives 17.9 mK, order 3 gives 21.2 mK: order 3 fits better in sample and extrapolates worse.
  • Clock offset +5.06 s, from CTD pressure against MR pressure — no thermal physics involved. This number is needed again in §6.
  • Geometry collinearity 0.889 — fires. dz (75.7 cm) and the residual lag are not separately resolved, because this deployment profiles in one direction only. Their combination is measured; the split is not.
  • Drift not significant: −1.8 ± 0.8 mK/day over 4.3 d, p = 0.084.

patch wrote 228 of 255 files; the other 27 are the data-less ones, skipped and counted.

05

Speed — the hard part, and the one thing that did not resolve

ε carries the through-water speed with ~U⁻⁴ leverage, so this matters more than anything else in the chain. That exponent was not assumed here — it was measured: the two speed arms differ by a factor 1.76 in ε at a speed ratio of 0.866, i.e. U−3.9.

The geometry, worked from the force balance

Let θ = elevation of the vehicle axis from horizontal, γ = the same for the velocity vector, α = angle of attack (all magnitudes).

Climb. Net buoyancy acts up. Along the path, B sin γ = D. Perpendicular, the balance needs a force of B cos γ pointing down-and-forward — the wing must supply the forward thrust that cancels drag's backward component, and on a climb the only perpendicular direction with a forward component is the downward one. A wing makes a downward-perpendicular force when flow arrives from above its chord, i.e. when the nose sits below the flight path. So γ = θ + α.

Dive. Mirror image: buoyancy down, perpendicular force up-and-forward, flow from below the chord, nose above the path — and again γ = θ + α.

Why this is worth stating

The glide path is steeper than the attitude on both legs, so U = |W| / sin(|pitch| + aoa_deg) is correct as written with aoa_deg positive. The intuitive answer — "the wing always meets the flow from below" — is true on a dive and false on a climb, and it produces a spurious dive/climb sign flip that fits the climb data almost as well. That wrong version returned a confident α = 1.9° ± 0.2°.

Why α could not be measured — and why that is structural

Because α and a U_EM scale error b enter with the same sign everywhere, sin(θ + α) = b · |W| / U_EM is one equation in two unknowns. They separate only through the curvature of sin() across a spread of θ.

The MicroRider runs on climbs only, by design — 208 of 208 sampled osu685 in-water files are climbs, and osu684 is 98.5% climbs. So no dive leg exists on either deployment, and this is a property of the measurement configuration rather than bad luck on two missions.

And recording both legs would not, by itself, fix it

With γ = θ + α on both legs, α enters identically each way. What separates α from b is spread in |θ|, nothing else. The Rutgers file has both legs — but flies them at 22° and 24°, and is correspondingly uninformative about the pair.

What is established

Corrected after the osu685 cross-check

Two claims in the first version of this note have been withdrawn. They are kept visible rather than quietly edited out, because both were plausible and both were wrong.

  • U_EM reads high relative to any physically admissible flight model. At θ = 43–46°, 150–600 dbar, steady glide, U_EM·sin θ / |W| = 1.116 — and since γ = θ + α with α ≥ 0, that ratio can never exceed 1. On osu685's identical footprint it is 1.044, so this is neither an osu684 artifact nor a consequence of the wing failure. This survives.
  • WITHDRAWN — the mechanism is not local flow acceleration. The EM head is at the front of the MicroRider, the MR sits on top of the glider, and the sensor tips are ahead of the glider's nose. A body accelerates flow alongside itself, not ahead of its stagnation point; ahead of a nose the flow is if anything decelerated, which would bias U_EM low — the wrong sign.
  • WITHDRAWN — "8–19% high" over-attributed the effect to one unit. The absolute correction is degenerate with α. What is well determined is the difference between units: at matched attitude, depth and flight state the two OSU EM units disagree by 7.0%.
  • The glider's own m_speed is unusable: it reports less than the measured vertical rate (0.219 vs 0.238 m/s). The flight computer's model is untuned for a MicroRider-carrying vehicle.

The EM calibration: tested, and rejected

A hypothesis stood here briefly: that the 2023-calibrated units carried a nominal slope, because SN 051 and SN 066 agree to 0.007% despite calibrations six months apart. Pulling the 2023-era coefficients killed it.

The decisive test: swap the EM, keep everything else

MR 435 flew EM SN 046 in 2023 and EM SN 066 in 2025 — the units were physically replaced after the nose cones flooded. Same MicroRider, same glider, matched footprint (150–600 dbar, θ 43–46°, climbs): excess 1.032 (2023) vs 1.043 (2025). The discrepancy survives an EM swap. It is not the flowmeter.

Roll is excluded too. Across deployments the excess rises with roll (+0.0118 per degree), which looks compelling — but within osu685, at fixed pitch and depth, it falls with roll (−0.0087 per degree). Opposite signs between and within is the signature of a lurking variable, and the roll bins covary with depth (229 → 493 dbar), which is the depth trend already known. Roll is a passenger.

So: the effect is real, reproduces across two instruments and two seasons, is stable over 89 days, and is not explained by the EM calibration, by roll, by non-equilibrium flight, by the dbar/s → m/s conversion, or by attitude bias. No mechanism currently survives. That is where it rests.

The perturb run

Four runs: {pre, post wing failure} × {flight, em}. One perturb-base.yaml is the source of truth and make_runs.py derives the four, so the ~180 shared lines cannot drift apart. 

files:  {trim: false, merge: false}   # merge would glue unrelated climbs together
profiles:
  direction: "up"        # the MR records CLIMBS: 1.12M steady in-water samples
                         # on climbs vs 16.7k on dives (98.5% / 1.5%)
hotel:
  time_offset: -5.06     # = -(fp07-cal clock_offset_s)
fp07: {calibrate: false} # the .p files already carry the in-situ coefficients
ct:   {align: false}     # the MR has no CT cell; the glider's is merged in
chi:  {use_epsilon: false}  # Method 2 — MR shear picks up vehicle vibration
The clock-offset sign

clock_offset_s is positive when the hotel timestamps run ahead of the instrument's, and hotel.time_offset is added to them — so time_offset = −clock_offset_s. Leaving it at 0 misregisters every merged CTD sample by 1.4 dbar at this deployment's 0.27 dbar/s climb rate, which is larger than the 1 m bin it lands in.

Attitude conventions — verified, not assumed

Over 1.31 M in-water samples against the glider's own inclinometer:

Incl_Y = -0.975 · m_pitch + 1.45°   r = -0.989   → a CLIMB is NEGATIVE Incl_Y
Incl_X = +0.861 · m_roll  - 2.53°   r = +0.961

so the QC pitch window is negative, and pitch_positive: "nose_down" — sign agreement between Incl_Y and dP/dt is the healthy pairing.

QC gates are sized to the vehicle, not to a default

This glider was badly ballasted from deployment: Incl_X ≈ 12° before the wing failure, ≈ 22–23° after. The usual roll abs_max: 10 would have rejected the entire deployment. 30° is used, which flags only excursions beyond the post-failure steady state.

The wing failure

Located to05:57:17UTC, 2025-02-06, at 50 m on a climb
Roll step17° → 25°inside one minute; settles at 29–31°
Climb pitch43° → 37°same minute

It is not a drift; it is a step. And the roll increase is climb-only — dives stay at 14–18° either side of it — which is diagnostic of a lift-surface change rather than a ballast shift.

Do not over-read the pre/post difference

Median ε falls by a factor 0.27 across the failure (flight-model arm). The vehicle changed and the deployment moved — from 500 m yos in one water mass to 750 m yos elsewhere. Water and platform changed together and are not separable from this dataset.

08

Open questions

  1. Run the MicroRider on a subset of DIVES. This is the whole recommendation, and it needs no unusual flight at all: osu685's glider already dives at |pitch| = 25.0° (5–95 pct 23.1–27.0) and climbs at 45.5° (32.5–49.1). That 20° separation, in normal operation, is ample to make the joint (α, EM-scale) fit identifiable. The spread already exists — it is simply not being recorded. Cost: MR power and storage on some dives. Payoff: the dominant term in the ε error budget, plus an in-situ EM calibration.
  2. The roll-dependent attitude disagreement. |Incl_Y| − |m_pitch| goes from −1.28° to −3.54° across the wing failure, tracking roll. That is far too large to be sensor accuracy (TrueNorth Revolution: ±0.2° precision, ±0.3° accuracy) or assembly tolerance (~±0.5°). The useful question for Rockland is what formula the MR firmware uses to convert its 2-axis tilt sensor into Incl_X/Incl_Y — an atan2(a_x, a_z) form is roll-dependent, an asin(a_x) form is not. More diagnostic than the accuracy spec.
  3. The depth structure of the discrepancy. On osu685 the excess falls monotonically from 1.12 at 55 m to 0.98 at 1000 m. Over a climb, pressure, speed (×1.5), temperature (21 → 5.5 °C) and — because the hull compresses differently from seawater, moving the centre of buoyancy — the trim and hence α all change together. Four candidate mechanisms are collinear along a glider profile and cannot be decomposed. Local acceleration is excluded (0.5% across the full range).
  4. Γ ≈ 0.6 against the canonical 0.2. Worth checking whether it survives the χ-side QC and whether it is depth-structured.
