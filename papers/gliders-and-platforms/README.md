# gliders-and-platforms

MicroRider-on-glider / AUV platform processing, and the through-water speed that
feeds it. ε from shear goes as a high power of that speed, so this group covers
both the published precedent for the deep-MicroRider window, noise-floor, and
telemetry choices and the literature behind `speed.method` in `rsi/speed.py`:
the steady-glide flight model, the angle of attack, and the documented
over-read of the AEM1-G `U_EM` flowmeter.

### What these mean for `speed.method`

- **The published EM-on-MicroRider over-reads are 7.5–17.6%**: a factor f means
  U_EM reads 1/f − 1 high, for f = 0.93–0.85 (plus Rockland's unpublished
  SeaExplorer case). The flight-vs-`U_EM`
  cross-check in `_flight_model_slow` warns only outside a median ratio of
  [0.8, 1.25], so it is silent on exactly the documented bias.
- **A scale factor derived from pressure and pitch is only as good as the AOA
  assumed.** At fixed pitch, sin(θ + α) = b·\|W\|/U_EM is one equation in two
  unknowns. Tanaka's Appendix E states the published version (coefficients and
  scale factor "cannot be determined uniquely without having another source of
  velocity measurement"), and their 0.85 (direct, against the ADCP) versus 0.90
  (via the 2019 AOA) on the same descents shows its size. An independent
  velocity separates them. That a spread of pitch angles also would, through
  sin()'s curvature, is this repository's reasoning, not a published result.
- **Both papers fit a gain only.** A zero offset c would instead give a factor
  1 − c/U that differs between legs flown at different speeds. Whether Tanaka's
  unexplained descent/ascent split is that signature is an open hypothesis in
  the AEM1-G work, not a published result, and a weak one: the ascent value's
  reference is not stated (above).
- **The steady-glide AOA falls with pitch**, so the `aoa_deg: 3.0` default
  cannot be right at every attitude. Solving
  a·α·tan(θ + α) = C_D0 + C_D1·α² with Tanaka's Slocum coefficients gives 4.25°
  at 23.7° against their measured 4.4°. That agreement holds by construction,
  since a was fitted to those data. The same coefficients give 3.2° at 30°,
  2.2° at 40° and 1.9° at 45°.
  - The 2019 coefficients give 1.8–2.0°, 1.2–1.3° and 1.0–1.1° at the same
    pitches with Merckelbach's C_D0 of 0.136–0.147.
  - With C_D0 = 0.164, the value used on Tanaka's Slocum, they give 2.2°, 1.5°
    and 1.2°.
  - C_D0 is glider-specific.
  - The only directly measured Slocum AOA in this group is Tanaka's, over about
    21–27° pitch; anything steeper is model extrapolation.

## Seaglider and Spray: flight models against independent references

The flight models of the other two glider families were tested against
references a Slocum MicroRider deployment rarely has: an acoustic tracking
range, an ADCP on an anchored ship, turns, and a deliberate range of pitch
angles. Their coefficients do not transfer to a Slocum, but their methods and
error budgets do.

| Paper | File | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Eriksen et al. (2001), "Seaglider: A Long-Range Autonomous Underwater Vehicle for Oceanographic Research," *IEEE J. Oceanic Eng.* **26**(4), 424–436. [doi:10.1109/48.972073](https://doi.org/10.1109/48.972073) | `Eriksen_2001_Seaglider_Hawaii.pdf` | The Seaglider flight model that the next three rows tune and test (lift proportional to attack angle, profile plus induced drag, buoyancy from the variable-buoyancy device), with a sign convention under which the glide angle exceeds the pitch angle in magnitude on both legs, as `speed.py` assumes. Its coefficients were fitted in Port Susan, Puget Sound, against an acoustic tracking range (four hydrophones, better than 1 m) with currents from an ADCP on the anchored work vessel, and again by minimizing modeled-minus-observed vertical velocity over 100 dive cycles (15 259 samples, residual s.d. **1.26 cm s⁻¹**). Profile drag came out **~30%** above the wind-tunnel value, which the paper puts down to the conductivity-sensor mount and antenna mast. In the tunnel a model conductivity sensor with ~2% of the frontal area carried **more than 25%** of the drag, evidence that appendages set the drag coefficient. |
| Frajka-Williams et al. (2011), "Determining Vertical Water Velocities from Seaglider," *JTECH* **28**, 1641–1656. In our reading this is the closest published analogue of the AOA / `U_EM`-scale degeneracy, with the same remedy. The authors add that "measured along-track or horizontal vehicle speeds from velocimeters or onboard ADCPs, even intermittently, would further constrain and improve flight model parameter estimates, especially the drag coefficient" (PDF p. 10), and this vehicle's extra sensors cost a drag parameter **70%** above the Seaglider default. |
| Rudnick, Sherman & Wu (2018), "Depth-Average Velocity from Spray Underwater Gliders," *JTECH* **35**, 1665–1673. [doi:10.1175/JTECH-D-17-0200.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/JTECH-D-17-0200.1) | `Rudnick_2018_Spray_Depth_Average_Velocity.pdf` | Applies the arithmetic of `speed.method: flight` to the Spray: glide angle = pitch + AOA·sign(pitch), with a fixed AOA of **3°** at 17° pitch, evaluated from pressure and pitch every 8 s. On first turns, when the gliders are clean, mean \|g\| is **1.01**, implying ~2.8° rather than 3°; biofouling raises the AOA later in a mission; the total rms accuracy is **0.014 m s⁻¹** for a fresh glider and ~0.017 m s⁻¹ over a mission. A sensor-free, dive-averaged check of through-water speed for any glider flying repeated sections, and a published case of a fixed AOA holding to about 1% on a clean vehicle. |

## Glider-mounted Doppler and bottom track

Velocity sensors carried by the glider itself. Through-water Doppler
velocity is what breaks the AOA / `U_EM`-scale degeneracy (Todd et al. 2017
and Tanaka et al. 2022, above). These three show what limits it on Slocums
and SeaExplorers: attitude updated every 4 s, payload clocks seconds off the
glider's, up-dive beam geometry, and bottom track, which measures velocity
over ground rather than through water.

[doi:10.1109/CWTM.2015.7098134](https://doi.org/10.1109/CWTM.2015.7098134) | `Thurnherr_2015_Explorer_ADCP_Slocum_LADCP_Shear.pdf` | TRDI Explorer ADCPs on Slocums, processed with the LADCP shear method and compared with a ship ADCP off California in 2014 (15 dives with ship data, 8 of them with bottom track). ## FP07 microstructure on Seagliders (MicroPod)

Rockland MicroPods on Seagliders, whose hull does not take a MicroRider. Here
ε comes from the FP07 (Batchelor fits, Thorpe scales), so these bear on the
chi side and on calibrating an FP07 against a glider CTD more than on speed.
Peterson & Fer (2014), under `chi-thermal/`, is the glider
temperature-microstructure counterpart.
